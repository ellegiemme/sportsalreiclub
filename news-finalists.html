Cape Verde

The one team neither World Cup finalist could beat in ninety minutes

July 20, 20264 min readSport Sal Rei Club
All news

When Argentina and Spain met in the final at the MetLife Stadium, they had one thing in common: neither had managed to beat Cape Verde inside the regulation ninety. For a nation of half a million people, it is a footnote worth framing.

Cape Verde arrived at their first World Cup as one of the smallest nations in the field and left it with a distinction none of the favourites could claim to have taken from them.

A goalless night against Spain

In the group stage, the Blue Sharks held the European champions to a goalless draw in Atlanta. Goalkeeper Vozinha was the decisive figure, keeping the sheet clean with seven saves — the performance that gave Cape Verde the first World Cup point in their history.

Argentina taken to extra time

In the Round of 32 in Miami, the reigning champions needed extra time to get past them. Messi opened the scoring, Deroy Duarte levelled just before the hour, and after Lisandro Martínez struck at the start of extra time it was Lopes Cabral who made it 2–2 again on 103 minutes. Only an own goal, with eleven minutes left, decided it.

What the numbers leave out

Argentina went on to eliminate Egypt, then Switzerland, then France’s conquerors on the way to the final. Spain came through Belgium and France. Between them the two finalists beat almost everyone they met — but not, within ninety minutes, the islands.

For clubs like ours, that is the part worth keeping. Discipline, organisation and courage travelled from island pitches to the biggest stage in the game, and held their own there.
