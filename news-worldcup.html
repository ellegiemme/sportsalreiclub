Cape Verde write history: the Blue Sharks reach the World Cup knockouts

July 6, 20265 min readSport Sal Rei Club
All news

On their first ever World Cup, Cape Verde went through Group H unbeaten and became the smallest nation ever to reach the knockout stage of the tournament. The run ended against Argentina in Miami — but only after the reigning champions had been taken to extra time.

For a country of roughly 525,000 people, spread across an Atlantic archipelago, simply reaching the World Cup was already a landmark. What followed went far beyond that.

Three draws, and a place in the last 32

Bubista’s side opened against Spain and held them to a goalless draw. A 2–2 with two-time world champions Uruguay followed, Cape Verde having led before being pegged back and then levelling again after the interval. A third clean-sheet draw, 0–0 against Saudi Arabia, was enough: three points, second place in the group, and qualification behind Spain without a single defeat.

It made Cape Verde the first debutant nation to reach the World Cup knockout phase since Slovakia in 2010, and — by population — the smallest ever to do so.

Taking Argentina to the limit

The Round of 32 tie against the defending champions was played in Miami on 3 July. Rather than sit deep, the Blue Sharks pushed forward. Lionel Messi put Argentina ahead before the half hour; captain Ryan Mendes released Deroy Duarte just before the hour and the equaliser arrived. The game went to extra time, where Lisandro Martínez restored Argentina’s lead in the first minute — only for Lopes Cabral to level again on 103 minutes. It took an own goal, eleven minutes from the end of extra time, to separate the sides. It finished 3–2.

The road that led there

Qualification had been sealed in October 2025 with a 3–0 win over Eswatini in Praia, in front of a full National Stadium. Cape Verde topped CAF Group D on 23 points, four clear of Cameroon, with goals from Dailon Livramento, Willy Semedo and Stopira sealing the night. By population, only Iceland has ever qualified as a smaller nation.

For every club on every island — Sal Rei included — the run is a reminder of what Cape Verdean football can reach. The players who wore the national shirt this summer began, like ours, on island pitches.
